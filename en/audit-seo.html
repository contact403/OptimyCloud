Definition

What is a Google SEO audit?

An SEO audit is a methodical review of your website from Google's point of view. The goal: understand why some pages don't appear, or appear too far down, in the search results, and know what to fix first.

Google crawls your pages, indexes them, works out what they are about, then ranks them against your competitors' pages. A blocker at any step costs you positions, so the SEO audit checks every link in the chain.

When you grant access, the analysis starts from your real data in Google Search Console: queries that generate impressions, the pages that receive them, average positions, indexing errors. The work is based on what Google actually sees of your site, not on guesswork.

This audit covers classic Google SEO. If you also want to appear in answers from ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or AI Overviews, see the GEO audit, which can be combined with this one.

Organic-search audit

What the SEO audit covers

Every factor that influences your Google ranking is examined in detail. Nothing is left to chance.

Core Web Vitals

LCP, INP, CLS: the performance metrics Google uses to rank your site. Analysis and recommendations for each indicator.

Schema markup

A review of your structured data (JSON-LD) for Google rich snippets: FAQ, reviews, products, breadcrumb, organization.

Indexing and crawl

Robots.txt, XML sitemap, orphan pages, redirects, 404 errors. We make sure Google can crawl and index every one of your pages.

Content and keywords

Title tags, meta descriptions, Hn structure, duplicate content, keyword cannibalization. Every page is analyzed individually.

Mobile experience

Google indexes the mobile version first. Responsive design, button sizes, readability, viewport: it all gets checked.

Security and HTTPS

SSL certificate, mixed content, security headers, HTTP-to-HTTPS redirects. An insecure site is penalized by Google.

Checklist

What gets checked, in detail

Here is concretely what is reviewed during the SEO audit. The list adapts to your site: a 10-page showcase site and a shop with several hundred products don't face the same issues.

Crawling and indexing

  • robots.txt and noindex directives
  • XML sitemap: presence, valid URLs, submission in Search Console
  • Pages excluded from the index and the reason Google gives
  • Canonical tags and duplicate URLs (www, trailing slash, parameters)
  • Response codes: 404, 500, redirect chains
  • Orphan pages with no internal link pointing to them

Performance and experience

  • Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS) on mobile and desktop
  • Image weight and format, lazy loading
  • Render-blocking scripts and stylesheets
  • Server response time and caching
  • Mobile display: viewport, readability, tap targets

Content and on-page tags

  • Title tags and meta descriptions: presence, length, duplicates
  • Heading hierarchy from H1 to H3
  • Thin pages and duplicate content
  • Keyword cannibalization: several pages targeting the same query
  • Match between Search Console queries and the page that ranks

Structure, structured data and links

  • Internal linking: key pages reachable in a few clicks
  • URL architecture and depth
  • JSON-LD structured data: organization, breadcrumbs, services, articles
  • Overview of inbound links (backlinks) and anchor texts
  • Consistent name, address and phone number for local SEO

Common issues

What's holding you back on Google

Here are the problems we run into most often during an SEO audit. If any of these sound familiar, your site needs a diagnostic.

Pages not indexed by Google

A misconfigured robots.txt, forgotten noindex tags, a missing sitemap. Google can't rank what it can't see.Fix: clean up robots.txt and noindex tags, submit a clean XML sitemap in Search Console, then request indexing of key pages.

Load times that are far too long

Uncompressed images, render-blocking JavaScript, a slow server. A slow site drives visitors away, and Google knows it.Fix: compress and resize images, lazy-load them, defer non-essential scripts, enable caching.

Duplicate or thin content

Pages with little text, identical titles, missing descriptions. Google favors unique, in-depth content.Fix: one search intent per page, unique titles and descriptions, and richer strategic pages.

Technical errors (404, 500, redirects)

Broken links, redirect chains, 500 server errors. These problems degrade the user experience and your ranking.Fix: direct 301 redirects to the right URL, updated internal links, root cause of server errors found (on WordPress, see WordPress troubleshooting).

No structured data

Without schema markup, Google can't display rich snippets (FAQ, reviews, prices). You lose visibility in the search results.Fix: add suitable JSON-LD markup (organization, breadcrumbs, services, articles), validated with Google's testing tools.

Site not mobile-friendly

Google uses mobile-first indexing. A site that doesn't display properly on a smartphone loses positions.Fix: responsive layout, suitable text and button sizes, checked on real smartphone screens.

Who it's for

Who is the SEO audit for?

Typical profiles

  • SMEs, tradespeople and freelancers whose site has been online for a while but brings in few leads from Google
  • Sites that lost traffic after a redesign, a migration or a domain name change
  • Companies preparing a redesign who want to know what to keep, fix or redirect

When it's too early

If your site has just gone live, a full audit is often premature: Google doesn't have enough data yet. The basics (indexing, tags, Search Console) can be checked during the free diagnostic. And if you don't have a site yet, website design builds these basics in from day one.

Frequently asked questions about the SEO audit

What is an SEO audit and why do one?

An SEO audit is a complete analysis of your website to identify everything holding back your visibility on Google. It covers the technical side (speed, indexing, security), the content (keywords, structure, tags) and authority (links, popularity). It is the essential first step before any organic-search strategy.

How much does an SEO audit cost?

The price of an SEO audit depends on the size of the site, the number of pages and the technical complexity. At OptimyCloud, every audit is quoted individually to fit your situation. Expect anywhere from a few hundred to several thousand euros depending on the scope. A free initial call lets us assess your needs.

How long does a complete SEO audit take?

A complete SEO audit generally takes between 5 and 10 business days depending on the size of the site. This includes the technical analysis, the content audit, the indexing review and writing the report with prioritized recommendations.

What results can I expect after an SEO audit?

After an SEO audit, you receive a detailed report listing the issues found, ranked by priority and by impact on your Google rankings. Sites that apply the recommendations see, on average, an improvement in their rankings within the following 3 to 6 months. The gains depend on the competition and on how the plan is implemented.

Does the SEO audit include fixing the issues?

The SEO audit includes the analysis, the report and the detailed action plan. The fixes can be handled by your own team by following the report, or entrusted to OptimyCloud as part of a separate engagement. Every recommendation is documented with the technical instructions needed to implement it.

What's the difference between an SEO audit and a GEO audit?

The SEO audit targets your position in Google's classic search results. The GEO audit targets your chances of being cited in answers generated by ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews. The technical foundations are shared (indexing, performance, structured data) and the two audits can be combined.

What I take on

DevOps and cloud consulting, from architecture to production

An engagement can cover one of these topics or all of them. Either way, what gets delivered runs in production and stays maintainable by your team.

AWS, GCP and Azure architecture

Architectures sized for your real workload: networking (VPCs, subnets, peering), compute, managed databases, storage and security. Every choice is documented and justified, including when the right answer is to keep things simpler.

AWS cloud migration and moving to Kubernetes

Migrating on-premise servers or VMs to the cloud, containerizing applications and moving them onto Kubernetes. Databases included, with a cutover and rollback plan agreed well before the big day.

Kubernetes: EKS, GKE, AKS

Building and taking over managed clusters, including EKS Auto Mode, which hands node management over to AWS. Ingress, autoscaling, secrets management, RBAC and version upgrades without downtime.

Infrastructure as code: Terraform, CDK

Your entire infrastructure described in versioned code with Terraform or AWS CDK: reusable modules, identical environments from staging to production, and code review before every change.

CI/CD pipelines

Automated build, test and deployment with GitLab CI, GitHub Actions or Jenkins. Runners hosted on your own infrastructure when needed, progressive rollouts and one-click rollback.

Observability and monitoring

Metrics, logs and alerts that are actually useful: Prometheus, Grafana, CloudWatch or Datadog depending on your context. Readable dashboards, and alerts that only wake someone up when it is justified.

Cloud governance and best practices

Account and project structure, naming and tagging conventions, security policies and a catalog of standardized services. A clear framework so every team can ship fast without reinventing the wheel.

Identity federation and centralized access

One directory to access the cloud console, the clusters and your internal tools: SSO through Entra ID, Amazon Cognito or IAM Identity Center. Fewer accounts to manage, and leavers handled in a single action.

Cost control from day one

Sizing, cost tags and budgets built into the architecture. If your bill has already gotten out of hand, the FinOps audit tackles that first.

Method

How an engagement runs

Four steps, each one signed off by you before moving on. Nothing changes in production without a written plan.

1

Audit of the current setup

Inventory of servers, applications, network flows, dependencies and pain points, with read-only access. We identify what needs to move, what can wait and where the risks are.

2

Design

Target architecture, choice of managed services, a migration strategy application by application and an estimate of running costs. You approve before anything gets built.

3

Build and migration

Infrastructure written in Terraform or CDK, CI/CD pipelines and monitoring, then a progressive cutover of applications with tests and a rollback plan at every step.

4

Handover and documentation

Operations documentation, incident runbooks and knowledge-transfer sessions with your team. You stay autonomous, with the option of ongoing support afterwards if you want it.

Frequently asked questions

AWS, GCP or Azure: which cloud should I choose?

All three providers cover what an SME needs. The choice mostly depends on your context: your team's skills, the tools already in place (a Microsoft 365 and Entra ID estate often points to Azure, heavy BigQuery use to GCP), the managed services you need and your data residency constraints. AWS still has the broadest catalog and it is my main area of expertise, but I recommend the cloud that suits you, not the one I prefer.

How do you keep downtime to a minimum during a migration?

The new infrastructure is built and tested alongside the existing one. Data is synchronized continuously where possible (database replication, incremental file copies), then the cutover happens application by application, in a window chosen with you, with a rollback plan written in advance. How long each application is unavailable depends on the application and its database; it is estimated during the design phase, before anything is committed.

Is Kubernetes a good fit for an SME?

Not always. For a handful of simple applications, services such as ECS, Cloud Run, App Runner or a well-automated VM are enough and cheaper to run. Kubernetes starts to pay off when you have several containerized services, several teams or real scaling needs. Managed offerings like EKS Auto Mode cut the operational burden a lot. As a CKA-certified Kubernetes consultant, I will tell you plainly whether it is the right tool for you.

What does infrastructure as code with Terraform actually bring?

Your infrastructure becomes code versioned in Git: every change is reviewed before it is applied, the history shows who changed what and why, and a full environment can be recreated identically. It removes hand-made configuration in the console, makes security audits easier and speeds up recovery after an incident considerably. Terraform works on AWS, GCP and Azure; AWS CDK is a good option if you are all-in on AWS and your team prefers TypeScript or Python.

Do you work alongside our in-house teams?

Yes, that is the most common setup. I plug into your tools (Git, ticketing, chat) and your rituals, pair with your developers or operations team and document as I go. The goal is for your team to be autonomous on everything that was built. I can also work on my own if you do not have infrastructure skills in-house yet.

Do you work remotely?

Yes, engagements are run remotely, in English or French. Cloud infrastructure is administered remotely anyway, and we work together over video, in your team chat and in your code repositories. Access is granted with the minimum permissions needed and revoked at the end of the engagement.
